The ocean cam: how we'd build it

Five stages, each with a clear stop point. We don't spend money until the stage before it checks out.

The stages

01

Confirm the view

We do

  • Assess the shot — composition, sun angles through the year, reflection risk
  • Confirm whether it's worth doing at all

You do

  • Photos from the window at 8am, noon, 5pm and after dark
  • Tell us which direction the window faces
Stop point: if there's no real ocean view from your side of the building, we stop here. Costs nothing to find out.
02

Placement & permissions

We do

  • Draft the request to building management
  • Spec both options — exterior/roof mount vs. through-glass
  • Define the network isolation requirements

You do

  • Send the request to property management
  • Check the lease for exterior equipment terms
  • Loop in whoever manages your IT
Stop point: the landlord's answer decides the design. Exterior mount is far better quality; through-glass is the fallback.
03

Camera & install

We do

  • Final camera spec and sourcing list
  • Written brief for the installer
  • Configure the stream and exposure profiles for day, sunset and night

You do

  • Approve the equipment budget
  • Book the installer and give building access
  • Have IT put the camera on an isolated network
Result: a live, stable stream. Nothing public yet.
04

The website page

We do

  • Build the ocean view page on gen5fertility.com
  • Player, Gen 5 branding overlay, mobile layout
  • Sunset countdown and daily timelapse replay
  • Separate tracking so this traffic doesn't distort your patient numbers

You do

  • Approve the page design
  • Provide logo and brand files
  • Decide on ambient audio (licensed, off by default)
Result: live and public on your site.
05

Getting it seen

We do

  • Submit to EarthCam and set up the YouTube channel
  • Write the local press angle
  • Build the search value into the page so it earns its keep

You do

  • Approve the public listing
  • Share it with your own network
Result: listed, discoverable, and pointing links back at Gen 5.

Three things to decide early

Advertising on the stream

Gen 5's own branding is straightforward and we'd build it. Selling ad space to medication companies is a different matter — it raises real regulatory questions for a medical practice, and needs your attorney's view before we design around it.

Sound

A microphone at your window records air conditioning and the freeway, not surf. Audio from other people's beach cams is their licensed content and isn't ours to use. The workable version is a licensed ambient track, off unless someone turns it on.

Cameras inside the clinic

A public feed from the IVF lab carries serious patient privacy exposure, and we'd advise against it. The better version of the same instinct: private, secure access for each patient to their own embryo timelapse. More meaningful to them, and genuinely differentiating.

What this is and isn't. This is a brand project, not a patient-acquisition one. It won't fill treatment cycles, and we shouldn't judge it on that. What it does is make Gen 5 memorable, earn coverage and links, and give people under real stress a moment of calm — which fits the practice better than most marketing ever will.
